# Additive cliff composition

## Regional refinement: retained ribs plus finite landforms

The vertical-weathered family and all previous baked maps are preserved. Its
coverage now varies spatially, including the retained slab map that was supplying
much of the dominant vertical rhythm. Quiet provinces suppress that contribution
without disabling micro stone. Overlapping masks favor ribs, ledges, buttresses,
quiet rock, pits and fractures rather than selecting one discrete material.

`build_cliff_landforms.gd` (seed 816394) adds eleven finite asymmetric shoulders
and thirteen finite diagonal/broken shelves. Each has an independently randomized
position and extent; shelf ends taper and do not wrap as continuous strata.
The two 512-square RGBAH mipmapped resources are `cliff_landforms.res` (body,
shelf height, underside cavity, lip) and `cliff_landform_slopes.res` (signed
derivatives). They add 5.33 MiB and four cliff texture fetches. They are reusable
periodic assets sampled at the two existing differing world-space projections,
not unique infinite-world textures. No previous assets were regenerated.

New shader controls (all range 0–2):

- `cliff_vertical_strength = 1.0`
- `cliff_buttress_strength = 1.0`
- `cliff_ledge_strength = 0.8`
- `cliff_collapse_strength = 0.7`

Isolation modes 6 and 7 isolate shelf and collapse contributions. F5 views 32–34
show ribs/ledges/quiet provinces (RGB), buttress coverage and collapse/apron
coverage. Collapse uses the same horizontal province field for wall coloration,
additional shelf/fracture contribution and existing talus-apron weighting. This
is correlated material treatment, not an upstream rockfall simulation or actual
fallen boulders. Cliff-lip exposure also varies with ledge/collapse regions.

Validation for this refinement:

- All five family/landform assets passed `check_cliff_families.gd` (bindings,
  dimensions, mipmaps, finite/nonempty channels).
- `preview_cliff_regions.gd` rendered close/wide, three debug views, and a second
  site without shader errors. Captures show quiet patches adjacent to retained
  ribbed patches and local broad relief; no new checker pattern was observed.
- `preview_macro_distance.gd` rendered 80/300/900/2400 m offsets. Macro coordinates
  contain no camera terms. This is a discrete distance check, not a continuous
  recorded flyback or exhaustive proof of invisible LOD transitions.
- Latest 1280x720 whole-viewport 180-frame mean: CPU 1.100 ms, GPU 4.926 ms.
  This is not a controlled isolated shader benchmark; do not compare the older
  timing below as a measured regression or speedup.

The reference appearance remains incomplete. Some broad shoulders are too smooth,
shelf shading is subdued on some faces, the cap remains geometrically continuous,
and debris is still material-only. This pass adds regional variety without
changing terrain generation, chunking, river systems or grassland materials.

The following documents the preceding foundation pass.

This pass preserves the sparse structural-recess maps and adds independently
weighted rock-mass, slab and weathered/pitted families. It does not change terrain
generation, streaming, water, lighting or grassland texturing. Cliff-edge material
classification is the only transition change outside the exposed rock shader.

## Composition

The existing cliff PBR material remains the base. Broad mass relief, local slab
relief, local pitting and existing sparse seams contribute together. Slowly varying
world-space region fields, convexity, concavity and drainage influence their
weights. Normal contributions are combined in world space and bounded. Related
rock albedos, weathering and roughness vary with the same regional composition.

Coordinates do not depend on camera distance. Fine normals fade with distance;
macro projection scale stays fixed. The existing two differently sized vertical
projections remain, with continuous orientation blending. This reduces repetition
but is not a mathematical guarantee of non-repetition across an infinite world.

New offline assets, generated by `build_cliff_families.gd` with seed 327183:

- `cliff_families.res`: 512-square RGBAH with mipmaps; mass, slab, pit and weather fields.
- `cliff_family_slopes_a.res`: RGBAH; signed mass and slab derivatives.
- `cliff_family_slopes_b.res`: RGH; signed pit derivatives.

Together these add approximately 6.67 MiB of texture payload and six texture
fetches per cliff fragment (three maps, two projections). No per-chunk maps or
runtime crack-network generation are added. Existing fracture assets are retained.

## Controls

Open `materials/terrain.tres` in Godot and expand Shader Parameters. Parameters
default in the shader; saving overrides in the material applies them on the next
run. Running terrain uses per-LOD material duplicates: live code-based adjustment
must update every entry in `Terrain._lod_materials`, not just the base material.

| Parameter | Default | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| cliff_mass_strength | 1.0 | Broad normal-based rock masses |
| cliff_slab_strength | 0.75 | Local slab relief and related rock blending |
| cliff_pit_strength | 0.45 | Local weathered cavity contribution |
| cliff_fracture_strength | 0.65 | Retained sparse recess/shelf contribution |
| cliff_micro_strength | 1.0 | Fine rock normal intensity, not albedo |
| cliff_region_metres | 180 | Regional family variation scale |
| cliff_macro_metres | 200 | Fixed world-space macro projection scale |
| cliff_crack_density | 0.55 | Coverage of fracture-favoring regions |
| cliff_cavity_density | 0.30 | Coverage of pitting-favoring regions |
| cliff_tonal_strength | 0.45 | Broad rock/weathering color variation |
| cliff_top_softness | 0.38 | Cliff slope-classification transition |
| cliff_bottom_softness | 0.65 | Talus integration transition |

Density changes coverage, not the number of paths inside the baked texture.
Zero density disables the corresponding regional contribution.

`cliff_layer_isolation`: 0 combined, 1 mass, 2 slab, 3 pit, 4 fracture,
5 micro. Isolation keeps the base rock/color backdrop while isolating relief
contributions; it is not a replacement production rendering mode.

F5 cycles debug views. New views 28–31 show combined family regions (red slab,
green weathered, blue fracture), slab region, weathered region and fracture region.
Existing cliff mask and geology views remain available. The test tool
`preview_cliff_composition.gd` captures all six isolation settings and the region
view at one identical camera position.

- Godot 4.6.1 Compatibility rendered the combined shader and all isolation views.
- `check_cliff_families.gd` passed dimensions, mipmap, finite-value and nonempty
  channel checks for all three new resources.
- Captured combined/isolation views at (-18400, 220, -2720), an oblique view,
  and distance views around (5600, 247.3867, 3840) at 80/300/900/2400 m offsets.
- Latest distance test at 1280 x 720 reported approximately 1.060 ms CPU and
  2.011 ms GPU, averaged over 180 frames. These are whole-viewport measurements,
  not isolated shader timings or a controlled before/after speed comparison.
- Combined and isolated renders show separate slab relief, quieter rock and
  localized weathering. No new checker pattern was observed in these captures.

## Remaining visual limitations

The photographic target is not yet achieved. Broad masses and weathered cavities
remain understated in some regions; normal-based structures cannot create real
silhouettes, overhangs or cast shadows. The new slab/pit fields are procedural
approximations, not scanned geology. Existing coarse cliff geometry and pale
talus/grass boundaries remain visible. No geometry or unrelated landscape system
was changed to conceal those limitations. These results are a tunable additive
implementation, not a claim that all visual success criteria have been met.
